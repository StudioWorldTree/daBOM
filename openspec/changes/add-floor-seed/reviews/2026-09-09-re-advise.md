## Blind take (written before opening design/tasks/prior review)

1. Pin: `floor` is a required key on `SeedItem`, no optional, no default. Typecheck is the first test that fails today.
2. Pin: one catalog-level test that imports `seedItems` and `seedBoms` with no DB. Every `seedBoms` parent is `assemble`; every other row is `buy`; nothing is `foundry`. Fails today because no row has the key.
3. Pin: the backfill at `seed.ts:71-76` is either removed or proven a no-op on the shipped seed. A silent override under an explicit field is the Why restated.
4. Pin: the upsert `set` list at `seed.ts:33-50` carries `floor`. Unreachable today (force deletes, non-force returns early) but a seed that upserts everything except this field is a trap.
5. Refuse: any seed row tagged `foundry`. Reserved by the living spec.
6. Refuse: deriving floor from `kind` or from `bom_lines` membership in code. Parent-by-accident is not assemble-by-intent. The tag lives in data.
7. Refuse: children under `t4000-som`, `rogue-t5`, `pyxis-*`, `rv1126b-turret`, `rv1126b-core`, `poe-switch-at`. Buy leaves. Die breakdown is a 409 today.
8. Tradeoff: required `floor` on the type costs 48 lines of `floor: 'buy'` noise across the catalog versus a DB default that hides omissions. Pay the noise.
9. Tradeoff: resin shells are printed in-house but priced. Empty `assemble` rolls up to zero and drops the quote. `buy` until resin plus labor are children.
10. Concern: the catalog test should also refuse a dangling SKU in `seedBoms`. FK catches it at insert, but the test is cheaper and runs without a DB.

## Compare

The amend answers 1 through 9 directly.

- Take 1: tasks line 2 and the delta both require `floor` on the type. `NewItem` from `$inferInsert` makes `floor` optional because the column has a default; intersecting with a required `'buy' | 'assemble'` literal makes it required and narrower. That is the right shape.
- Take 2: design "Proof the crib is not silent" is the test as I would write it. Verified it fails today: `catalog.ts` has zero occurrences of `floor`, so `row.floor === 'assemble'` is `undefined === 'assemble'` for all eight parents. Verified the eight parents named in design are exactly the eight distinct `parentSku` values in `seedBoms`, and every one has a catalog row.
- Take 3: backfill stays. Its `where` is `distinct parent_sku from bom_lines`, and after seed that set equals the `seedBoms` parents, which the catalog test pins to `assemble`. Seed only runs on an empty DB or after force-delete, so no other rows ever reach it. Proven inert by the test, as design says.
- Take 4: tasks line 5 adds `floor` to the upsert set. Hygiene, done.
- Take 5: `foundry` removed from the tasks list of allowed values, type excludes it, test refuses it. Three doors on one reserved value; fine.
- Take 6, 7: design "Data, not a kind rule", proposal, and delta hold T4000 and the named leaves as `buy` with no children. Steer decided this. Unchanged from the first pass and correct.
- Take 8: paid. 48 rows, 40 `buy`, 8 `assemble`.
- Take 9: resin shells named `buy` in design, tasks, and delta with the quote reason. Closed.

Take 10 is not in the change. Not blocking; see notes.

## Does the fix close the prior hole

Yes. The prior send-back was one hole: no acceptance could fail before and
pass after. Two things now fail today with no product change:

- `npm run check` once `SeedItem` requires `floor`, on all 48 rows.
- The catalog-level test on `seedItems`/`seedBoms`, on all 8 parents and all 40 leaves.

The existing DB-level assertion at `app.test.ts:120` stays as the second
door and is correctly no longer the acceptance for this change.

compose-schema learning is that a bare default would have exploded seeded
kits to nothing. With the catalog test in place the net cannot mask a
missing tag on the shipped seed, so keeping it is free. I would have
deleted it, but the amend's position is defensible and pinned.
